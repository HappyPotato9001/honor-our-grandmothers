In Plain Sight

A Memorial to Elderly Asian Women Targeted During COVID-19: Intersectional Memory, Elder Protection, and Solidarity

During COVID-19, race, gender, and age converged to make elderly Asian American women uniquely vulnerable. Daytime street assaults happened in plain sight; verbal harassment followed them on buses and sidewalks; and many retreated into homebound isolation when neighbors and systems failed to protect them.

Enter the Memorial
Zone 1 · Historical Roots

From the Page Act to Perpetual Foreigners

Anti-Asian elder vulnerability did not begin in 2020. Exclusion laws and detention systems framed Asian women as suspect outsiders and denied families rooted belonging for generations.

Zone 2 · Ideological & Institutional Oppression

Scapegoating & Language-Access Barriers

Pandemic rhetoric revived 19th-century disease tropes while municipal systems still failed to speak our grandmothers' languages—leaving elders unprotected in plain sight.

Zone 3 · Interpersonal & Intersectional Harm

Race, Gender, and Age on Urban Streets

Stop AAPI Hate research and Bay Area street realities show how anti-Asian racism intersected with sexism and ageism—concentrating violence against women and elders in daylight, in plain sight.

Behind Closed Doors

When harassment happens in plain sight, the wound does not stop at the front door. Fear settles into silence, hyper-vigilance, and isolation inside the home.
