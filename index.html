Digital Memorial & Intersectional Archive

Honor Our Grandmothers

A Digital Memorial on Anti-Asian Hate Toward Elderly Women During COVID-19

For more than a century and a half, laws, media narratives, and street violence have converged on Asian women and elders—compounding race, gender, age, and language barriers. This archive traces that history from the 1875 Page Act to Bay Area Chinatowns today, honoring our grandmothers' resilience and mobilizing community care.

Zone 1 · Historical Foundations

From the Page Act & Exclusion to Perpetual Foreigners

Anti-Asian elder vulnerability did not begin in 2020. Federal statutes and detention systems framed Asian women as suspect outsiders and denied Asian families rooted belonging for generations.

Zone 3 · Interpersonal & Intersectional Impact

Race, Gender, and Age on Urban Streets

National reporting and Bay Area street realities reveal how anti-Asian racism intersects with sexism and ageism—concentrating visible and physical violence against women and elders.

Stop AAPI Hate Finding

2.3× Higher Reporting Rate Among Women

2.3×

Asian American women reported hate incidents at 2.3 times the rate of men (68% vs. 29% across landmark 2020–2021 national reporting; 11,400+ total reports 2020–2022).

Bay Area Street Reality

Elder Assault Surge in SF & Oakland Chinatowns

60+

Elders aged 60+ experienced heightened physical assault rates during 2020–2022 surges in San Francisco and Oakland Chinatowns, turning routine grocery walks and transit trips into moments of fear.
